This project applies forecasting and predictive modelling techniques to the cleaned quarterly financial dataset prepared in Weeks 2 and 3 for TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, and Wipro. The objective was to create a transparent, reproducible short-horizon forecasting process that could estimate future quarterly revenue and operating-margin levels while explicitly measuring forecast error and modelling limitations.

Because the dataset contains only 13 common quarterly observations per company, the modelling approach was intentionally conservative. Candidate methods included a naive last-value baseline, linear drift, damped Holt exponential smoothing, ARIMA(1,1,0), and a low-parameter AutoReg(1) model. Rather than selecting a method based on in-sample fit, I used a time-ordered validation design in which the final three observed quarters were held out from the training sample. Each candidate was evaluated on the unseen periods using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), with MAE used as the primary selection criterion.

After model selection, the chosen model for each company and target was refitted using all available observations through Q1 FY27 and used to generate a four-quarter forecast covering Q2 FY27, Q3 FY27, Q4 FY27, and Q1 FY28. The analysis documents the variables, modelling assumptions, validation steps, error results, selected specifications, and the implications of model risk.

A major limitation is the short historical sample, which makes highly complex machine-learning approaches inappropriate and reduces confidence in long-horizon forecasts. External variables such as exchange rates, contract wins, attrition, headcount, macroeconomic conditions, and industry demand were not added because the Week 2 dataset did not provide a sufficiently aligned historical feature set. Therefore, the model output is presented as a transparent statistical baseline for scenario analysis rather than a precise earnings prediction.

Overall, the project strengthened practical skills in time-series modelling, temporal validation, model comparison, forecast-error analysis, reproducible Python workflows, and the communication of uncertainty in financial forecasting.
